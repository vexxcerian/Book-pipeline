## 1. THE CLOSING SHAPE

**Chosen shape: the protagonist involuntarily beginning to execute the method he refused —
stopped mid-way, uncommented — and then performing a small act of ordinary courtesy on top
of it.**

The last movement is Vexx working out, on his own, where a stranger's housing allocation
would be published and how you would get a man's leave out of him in the first ten minutes.
It is Zeus's method, in Vexx's own head, forty minutes after he said no twice. Then: *"He had
got that far before he stopped."* Then he turns his cup upside down on the rack the way you
leave a thing for whoever comes in after you, kills the light, and goes up.

**Why it is distinct from the eight spent:**
- Not Ch.2's dramatic irony — the reader knows exactly what Vexx knows, no more.
- Not Ch.3's scene beat with another character — Aglaope has already left the room.
- Not Ch.4's interior equation — an equation *resolves*; this is a procedure that stops
  halfway and is never completed, and the final image is an action, not a thought.
- Not Ch.5's object handled in the dark — the cup is handled in the light, and the act is
  social rather than private.
- Not Ch.6's misdirected answer to a stranger — no one is present.
- Not Ch.7 or Ch.8's document. **There is no paper on the last page.** The outline's *"two
  rooms, four people, no paper"* was held.

**Momentum.** One question closed (will Vexx take the offer — no). Two opened, both sharper:
the badge log that goes up on Monday with the water and the lights (the Ch.10 seed, planted
by Zeus as a courtesy rather than a threat), and the fact that Vexx now knows the method well
enough to start running it without deciding to.

## 3. THE BEAT SUBVERSION — did the ally stay un-tempting

Zeus is never once selling. The order of the scene was built so that he cannot be read as
recruiting:

- He has been in the room two hours before Vexx arrives, doing a puzzle, and **does not raise
  the subject.** Vexx raises the puzzle. Zeus raises the cook.
- The offer is delivered in the same voice as the guitar peg and the coffee scale, and the
  narration says so once: *"He said all of it at one speed, unhurried, like a man giving
  directions to a road he drives every week."*
- He does not argue when Vexx refuses. He does not restate. He does not leave a door open.
  **He says "I'd have been sorry if you'd taken it"** — badly, into the middle of a
  conversation about coffee, thirty seconds after turning a cup a half-turn on a table for no
  reason. Vexx sees it and says nothing, and the narration says he saw the shape of a thing to
  say and left it where it was.
- Zeus **never asks why**, anywhere. When Vexx asks *him* why — *"Why."* — he says *"Ask a
  different question,"* which is the only refusal in the chapter that costs nothing.
- The one thing he does push is the *warning*: the terminal logs the badge, and it goes up on
  Monday. That is the trap doing its work without anyone intending it. The lawful route leaves
  a mark; the terrible one doesn't. Nobody says that sentence out loud.

**Vexx's refusal is not noble.** It arrives before a decision would ("like a hand coming off a
hot pan"), and then he *keeps asking* — *"How long does it take. Finding out what a man's
protecting."* — gets the answer (*"Less, if he has children"*), and has to say *"Don't."* The
"don't" is aimed at himself. Then his hands go flat on the table edge and come off again.

**The line engineered to stop the reader cold:** *"Less, if he has children."* Said kindly, in
the peg voice, by the most considerate person in the chapter.

## 10. WHAT I THINK IS WRONG WITH THE BRIEF

1. **The dispatch says the outline's beat 8 has the notebook "with him in the archive," and the
   outline also says "Device presence: None ... no paper."** Those are in tension. I resolved it
   by keeping the notebook physically present and **never showing the reader a line of it as a
   document** — its contents arrive as three fragments of Vexx's memory of reading it, not as
   quoted text. If a later pass reads the *depot, no answer* fragments as a fourth consecutive
   document device, cut them to a single unquoted sentence rather than removing the notebook.
2. **`character-bible.md` and the manuscript disagree about HOLST.** The bible says he "is never
   given a first name"; the author's Ch.7 prints *STAFF SERGEANT DERIC A. HOLST* in a casualty
   entry. Pre-existing, not introduced here, but it will bite whoever writes the RECLAMATION
   payoff. The prose should win, as it did with the private-tally carve-out.
3. **The "≥40w narration" floor has no ceiling**, and this chapter sits at 15.6% against the
   author's measured 11.6–16.3%. That is inside his range by luck rather than by design: the
   fix for a low narration median is merging short sentences, which drives the long-sentence
   percentage up at the same time. A future chapter that has to make a large median correction
   will overshoot the author's top end and nothing will catch it.
4. **Nine chapters, nine closing shapes, is close to exhausted.** After Ch.9 the unspent
   inventory is thin — a sound, a refusal to act, a line of dialogue that lands wrong, a jump
   in time. Ch.10 should be given its closing shape in the dispatch rather than asked to find
   one.
