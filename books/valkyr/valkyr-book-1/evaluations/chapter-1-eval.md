## Where the bar sits (this is the point of evaluating a locked chapter)

Four things in Ch.1 are the standard the rest of the book is being measured against. Name them
so Ch.6–26 can be checked against something concrete rather than "match the voice."

1. **Feeling is displaced onto an object or a procedure, never reported.** The chapter's
   biggest emotional moment is a five-word paragraph about a casket. There is no sentence
   anywhere in Ch.1 of the form *X felt Y.* (Ch.2, 3 and 5 each break this — see those evals.)
2. **The long sentence earns its length by putting the payload last.** *"…the particular
   clatter of a mess hall at 1100 — receding behind him like a tide going out."* Then, in the
   next breath, the four-word correction: *"He got something closer to vertigo."* Long-then-
   short is the engine; the short sentence is where the meaning is.
3. **The retrospective narrator is used exactly where the reader can't yet do the work
   himself** — the Jameson flinch, the sealed memory. It is never used to re-tell the reader
   something already established.
4. **Rx's humor is load-bearing, not comic relief.** *"In the flesh. Poor choice of words.
   Sorry."* proves two men share a private language, in nine words, at the exact moment the
   reader needs proof that the AI is a person.
