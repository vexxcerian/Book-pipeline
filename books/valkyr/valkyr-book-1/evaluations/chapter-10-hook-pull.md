## 1. The opening — kept, sharpened

**Mandated first line untouched:**

> Eleven forty, room 2-14, north end of the operations block — nothing on the summons about what for.

**Hook type: QUESTION.** Operative verb: **had come up** (the summons acts; the man does not
yet). The gap is administrative and therefore worse than a dramatic one — a document with a
time, a room, no name, no reason, and a clause reference pointing two authorizations above
anything he holds. It is answered inside the chapter (the audit) and the answer is not the one
he prepared for.

Score **9/10** — information gap 3/3, emotional pull 2/3, voice 2/2, connection 2/2. Above the
threshold before this pass and above it after; I did not replace it.

What I sharpened, and why it counts as sharpening rather than taste: the second sentence was a
**64-word accumulation** carrying three separate things — what the summons said, what he did
about the clause, and where he carried it. It deferred *"He had assumed Merrick"* — the beat
that actually supplies the dread — to fourth position inside one very long breath. Split into
the inventory, the fragment and the action, the paragraph now delivers the document, the failed
lookup and the man moving as three discrete beats, and Merrick arrives one beat sooner. (This is
also breath edit 1; see §4.)

**No echo of Ch.9's close.** Ch.9 ends in a mess hall at four in the morning, a cup left upside
down on the rack, a man going up. Ch.10 opens on a timestamp and a routing block at eleven forty.
Different hour, different register, no shared image, and Ch.9's opening device (unattributed
dialogue three words in) is not reused.

## 2. The single join

The chapter is one room for ~4,050 words across nine internal breaks, then the corridor. Eight of
the nine breaks are cuts *inside the same scene* (and one — the mess hall — is a cut inside
Vexx's head). **There is one real join**, and it is load-bearing:

> "It is," Dessen said, in the voice of a woman filing something.
> * * *
> The elevator at the north end takes its time, and Jameson was still standing at it when Vexx came out of the room…

It works, and it is left alone. The room exits on **Dessen's own unheard grievance** — the
transfer she raised, that nobody picks up — and the corridor opens on the man who *was* heard,
still standing there. The verb tense does the hinge: *takes its time* is the habitual present,
the book's one permitted step out of past tense, and it lands the reader in the corridor before
the sentence tells them who is in it. Momentum across the join: **strong**.

## 4. The pull

**The closing move is untouched**, per the brief — the tenth distinct shape: an unremarked act of
preparation *for* the antagonist. Last verb: **learned them**.

**Type: EMOTIONAL GUT PUNCH, delivered as an action and left unglossed.** Not a cliffhanger, not
a question plant, not an ominous promise — nothing is withheld from the reader and nothing is
promised to them. The turn of the page is bought by the fact that **Dessen is right and Vexx did
it**, and that the man who did it has just spent an unwatched minute memorizing barometric
figures so he will have something to say to the man who killed his brother, using the compartment
built into him at fourteen *"for holding things he would have to say back quickly, correctly,
under pressure, to a man who would be pleased with him."* The dread is moral and it is entirely
reader-supplied. Score **9/10** — forward pull 2/3 (deliberately; it is not an information pull),
emotional charge 3/3, unpredictability 2/2, promise 2/2.

**Confirmed: no line converts the moral pull into a plot hook.** Every forward-leaning
construction in the narration was pulled and read individually (`would`, `will`, `going to`,
`never`, `until`, `eventually`, `one day`, `by then`, `in time` — eight hits, all listed below),
and the four flash-forward tells were searched across the **whole chapter, not just the ends**
(*he would understand later*, *he did not know it yet*, *it would be some time before*, *what he
could not have known was*, plus *would later*, *would learn*, *would come to*, *not know it yet*,
*years later*, *would think about*). **Zero hits in narration.** The single string match in the
file is inside Dessen's speech — *"if it turns out later that you do, that'll be a different
conversation, a worse one"* — which is a serving officer stating her own procedure in the present,
not a narrator standing downstream and reporting back.

The three sentences worth naming, because each is the kind that usually hides the fault:

- *"filed where nobody would have cause to open it again … where it would still be tonight"* —
  Vexx's present knowledge of a filed document and of his own bedside drawer. He is not told what
  happens to either; he knows where they are.
- *"the first lie he had told in that room"* — a count of what has already happened, not a promise
  of a second.
- *"…and had said almost nothing since the audit-room door opened"* (Rx) — Vexx noticing a silence.
  The narration does not explain it, and Vexx does not flag it. Correct: the reader supplies the
  chill, the scene supplies none.

**Nothing anywhere in the chapter is narrated outside Vexx's perception.** No sentence in the file
can be prefixed with *little did he know*.

## 5. The breath repair — five edits, every one a split

Flagged before: `BREATH narration median 18.5 > 18.0` and `>=40w 20.4% > 16.5%`. Author's measured
narration: median 14.0–17.5, ≥40w 13.3–16.2%.

**Method: split, never trim.** Nothing was cut. Net word change **+2**. All five splits were made
in sentences that were long because they carried **two or three separate thoughts**; the author's
appositive-rhythm sentences — the pen writing the apology at the speed of the insult (52w), the
tag in the drawer (66w), *there it was, under the breastbone* (48w), the man waiting in the
corridor (65w), the closing sentence (82w) — were identified and **left entirely alone**.

A note on the arithmetic, because the obvious move is wrong: splitting a 60-word sentence into two
30-word halves **raises** the median, since both halves land above it. Every split below was
therefore cut so that **one piece falls at or below 17 words**, which is what actually moves the
median down. That is why the splits are asymmetric.

**1 — opening paragraph, 64w → 27w + 17w + 21w.** Three thoughts: the summons, the failed lookup,
the carrying.
> **before:** …the word ATTEND set in capitals, a clause reference that Vexx looked up on the walk down, understood no better for having looked, then carried into the stairwell with him because it pointed at a schedule sitting two authorizations above anything he held.
> **after:** …the word ATTEND set in capitals. A clause reference that Vexx looked up on the walk down, understood no better for having looked. He carried it into the stairwell with him because it pointed at a schedule sitting two authorizations above anything he held.

**2 — the notebook, 69w → 21w + 31w + 16w.** Three thoughts: no paper in this building, the book,
the pen. Isolating the third gives the chapter's stated emotional anchor its own sentence.
> **before:** Vexx had not seen paper in that building outside of the laminated card taped to the terminal down in the annex — this was a bound book, cloth spine, a corner gone soft like a much-handled card, lying open under her left hand while her right went on at a steady unhurried rate, the pen making a sound in the quiet room like a fingernail going down a seam.
> **after:** Vexx had not seen paper in that building outside of the laminated card taped to the terminal down in the annex. This was a bound book, cloth spine, a corner gone soft like a much-handled card, lying open under her left hand while her right went on at a steady unhurried rate. The pen made a sound in the quiet room like a fingernail going down a seam.

**3 — the audit begins, 45w → 8w + 38w.** A statement and a list of absences, welded at an em-dash.
> **before:** She began where they teach you to begin, without any of the small theater Vexx had been braced for — no folder pushed across the table, no pause held for effect, no question asked twice over in a different tone to see whether the answer moved.
> **after:** She began where they teach you to begin. There was none of the small theater Vexx had been braced for — no folder pushed across the table, no pause held for effect, no question asked twice over in a different tone to see whether the answer moved.

**4 — the lie, 46w → 30w + 16w.** The taxonomy of the lie, and the body knowing it. Two thoughts,
and the second is the better sentence standing alone. Sentence-initial *And* is the author's own
(Ch.1 ×2, Ch.2 ×1) and preserves the `and` count exactly.
> **before:** …a different animal altogether from a withheld one, and his body knew the difference about a second before his mouth caught up with it.
> **after:** …a different animal altogether from a withheld one. And his body knew the difference about a second before his mouth caught up with it.

**5 — Jameson's entrance, 50w → 14w + 36w.** The man, then the weather he brought in with him.
See §3: this is the reading-speed repair as much as the breath repair.
> **before:** Jameson had a data pad in one hand, his cap in the other, and he had come from outside — the shoulders of his uniform dark with it, weather standing in the corridor behind him…
> **after:** Jameson had a data pad in one hand and his cap in the other. He had come from outside — the shoulders of his uniform dark with it, weather standing in the corridor behind him…

## 7. Considered and declined

- **Splitting the 54-word summons sentence** (*"That was the shape a summons had in his head
  now…"*). It reads as two thoughts and would have split cleanly, but the metrics were already
  inside band at five edits and the only available cut deletes an `and`. Left long.
- **Splitting the 70-word *"He had known the shape of the third"*** and the **66-word drawer
  sentence** at the second reading of the finding. Both are appositive-rhythm sentences that need
  the room — the first is a man watching a question arrive with nothing behind it, the second is
  the whole moral weight of the chapter in one breath. Neither is carrying two thoughts; they are
  carrying one thought a long way. Left long, and they are why ≥40w sits at 14.8% rather than
  lower.
- **Doing anything to §5 (what it cost Corwin).** Median 7.0, no long sentence, zero adjustment.
  It is the best-paced passage in the chapter.
- **The ending's callback to the opening** — the fire-door lip where the money ran out, in the
  first paragraph and in the last sentence. Flagged as the standard symmetrical-ending
  anti-pattern and **kept**: it is not structural neatness here, because the two instances say
  opposite things. In the opening he walks the corridor carrying a rehearsed answer to a question
  nobody will ask him. In the closing he stands in the same corridor rehearsing an answer nobody
  has asked for at all, on purpose, for the man who killed his brother.
- **Strengthening the corridor coda's forward pull.** Declined, deliberately. The brief is right
  that the pull here is dread and not information; anything added at the elevator would have been
  the plot hook this chapter is specifically built to refuse.
