The outline promised **"six pairings, six distinct interview textures."** Three of the six
have no texture at all — they have a précis. **Zeus has zero lines of dialogue in his own
recruitment scene.** Paladin has one. Their characters are delivered entirely by narrator
assertion (*"the kind of soldier other soldiers instinctively formed up behind"*), which is
the chapter telling you who these people are three chapters before it will need you to
believe it.

And the rhythm reports the same thing independently:

`voice-dna.md` sets the floor at **≥25% of sentences at ≤6 words** and states that *"a new
chapter whose sentence-length histogram has a fat middle is off-voice, no matter how good the
prose is."* Ch.2 is at 18.1% with the fattest middle in the book. That is not a coincidence
and it is not a taste judgment: **montage prose has no short mode, because summary sentences
are all medium.** Fixing the montage fixes the histogram. They are the same defect.

## Anti-AI Scan — reported only where it hurts the page

- **The four-crutch stack (F1).** One paragraph runs all four of the manuscript's at-risk
  constructions back to back: *"He filed the thought away the way he'd filed away Rx's silence
  in Jameson's office. **Not yet a problem. Just a shape on the horizon he didn't have the
  vocabulary to name.**"* Three consecutive sentences, three separate calcified moves, plus a
  self-referential "the way he'd filed" that makes the tic visible to the reader.
- **"Filed away" x5 in one chapter** (`filed the quiet away as nothing` · `filed the thought
  away the way he'd filed away` · `filed away, the way everything got filed away`). The
  chapter's **last line** and the paragraph above both end on it. `voice-dna.md` puts this
  phrase AT CAP for the whole book at 7 uses; Ch.2 alone spends 5.
- **#10 Described emotions:** *"Vexx felt something in his chest ease, just slightly."* This is
  the chapter's declared emotional surprise and it is reported rather than staged. Ch.1 has
  zero such sentences in 3,911 words. See F4.
- **Clear and working:** em-dash 10.9/1k, similes, long sentences. Not findings.

**Hostile:** "Vexx meets the man who murdered his brother and the narrator tells me so in
sentence 12. Why should I stay tense for 24 more chapters?" — the answer is that the tension is
Vexx's, not the reader's, and that is a legitimate design; but it needs the cast to be people
so the reader has someone to be tense *for*. Half the cast isn't there yet.
**Casual Reader:** 7.5/10. Likes Goliath immediately, likes Jameson, cannot name Zeus or
Paladin by the end of the chapter.

## Strengths to PRESERVE
- **The Jameson introduction, entire.** This is the calibration standard: warm, specific,
  gets the syllables right, and never once readable as sinister. Do not touch a word.
- **The Phantom slip and Goliath going still.** The best emotional engineering in Ch.2–5: the
  reveal is delivered by an AI's hesitation, the operator's response is a physical stillness,
  and the POV character is on a balcony where he can't hear it. Three good decisions stacked.
- **The handwritten addendum.** Anchor of the chapter.
- **Rx's silence used twice** — noticed, dismissed, noticed again, dismissed again.

## PATH TO 8.5
- **Characters 7.5 → 8.5 requires:** F2. One converted scene (Zeus) plus one dramatized beat
  each for Gaia and Paladin. The unlock condition is that a reader can, at the end of Ch.2,
  quote one sentence *spoken by* each of the six operators. Currently they can do it for two.
- **Pacing 7.5 → 8.5 requires:** the same surgery. The 754-word summary block is the only
  slack stretch in the chapter; converting it restores the short mode and drags the median
  sentence back from 16.5 toward the book's 12. Check the histogram after, not before.
