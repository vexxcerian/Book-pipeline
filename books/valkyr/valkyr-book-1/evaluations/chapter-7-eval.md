### Has it hit the metric while still sounding like a machine? No — and here is the proof that it is inhabited rather than gamed.

A calibrated chapter can hit an em-dash count by sprinkling dashes. Ch.7 does not sprinkle. **All seven of its ≥40-word sentences use the author's specific move** — the self-interruption, the appositive catalogue dropped inside the dashes, the emotion landing after the interruption. Compare, directly:

> **Ch.1 (author, locked):** *"He remembered walking out of that building for the last time in a uniform that already felt like it belonged to someone else, the base noise around him — dropship engines spooling, drill instructors barking somewhere out past the motor pool, the particular clatter of a mess hall at 1100 — receding behind him like a tide going out."*

> **Ch.7 (pipeline), first prose sentence in the chapter:** *"The table in his kitchen was not a kitchen table — a folding utility trestle, hollow-legged, with a burn in the laminate at one corner that had been in it when he carried it up the stairs — and at a quarter past eleven at night it held a pad, a plate he had not touched, and a dog tag with a dent in it."*

Same architecture: assertion → dash → appositive catalogue with a history buried in it → dash → the payload deferred to the tail. The Ch.6 version of this sentence would have chained five verbs on `and` and landed nothing. It is gone.

And the move recurs where it is hardest to fake — under pressure, mid-clause:

> *"Vexx put his thumb on the serial. He could find it in the dark — had, more than once, in a drawer beside a bed, with the light off — and he switched the lamp on over it anyway."*

That is the author's nervous interruption used as a *control break*: the dash pair holds the past, and the sentence's last five words undo it. No pipeline chapter before this one produced that.

### **Answer to the author's question, stated plainly: one author.**

A reader going Ch.5 → Ch.6 → Ch.7 meets one author. The Ch.6 seam was audible-in-principle as *calmness*; Ch.7 is not calm. It is the most nervous prose in the manuscript after Ch.1 itself, and it is nervous in the author's own idiom rather than a simulation of it.

### But the seam has moved one layer down, and I will not declare victory.

Three metrics are still outside the author's range, and two of them are the *same divergence in a new coat*:

1. **Breath. `≥40w` at 8.8 % and median 11, against an author floor of 11.7 % / 13.** Ch.6 was 7.0 % / 11. Both pipeline chapters are shorter-breathed than every author chapter including the author's own 1,902-word short chapter (Ch.5: median **17**, ≥40w 12.8 %). Ch.7 has an alibi the outline wrote for it — *"Fragmented. Log lines and white space. Shortest paragraphs in the book"* — and it passes the voice-dna histogram gate. **The alibi is good and it is single-use.** If Ch.8 (6,200 words, immersive, decelerated) comes in at median 9–11, the seam is not punctuation and never was: it is that the pipeline reaches for the long accumulating mode less often than the author does, and the em-dash fix was treating a symptom.
2. **`somebody`/`nobody` at 3.2/1k.** The Ch.6 finding recommended a ≤3.0/1k gate. Ch.6 post-revision sits at 3.4 and Ch.7 at 3.2. **Not closed.** In mitigation, all five Ch.7 instances are load-bearing (*"every line on it somebody's boots"*, *"Nobody hid this"*, *"Somebody had written photo?"*, *"Nobody's asked me to keep it"*) and the chapter is *about* anonymous agency. The breach is marginal and I would not spend an edit on it here — but it should be recorded as unfixed rather than quietly passed.
3. **Numeric tokens at 46.4/1k — 3.4× the author's ceiling, and the new outlier.** Ch.6 was already at 16.9. See §Pattern 13 and §Device Bleed. Most of it is diegetic and the chapter's best structural joke depends on it (bury the one number that matters in a night of numbers that don't). But the trend line Ch.5 4.2 → Ch.6 16.9 → Ch.7 46.4 is a direction, and directions become fingerprints.

**Recommendation to the pipeline (root, per the UPDATE RULE):** add `≥40w sentence %` and `median sentence length` to `voice-dna.md` §0 as a per-chapter budget alongside the four §THE SEAM metrics, with an explicit exemption clause for declared punch chapters. Ch.7 should be recorded as *exempt and passing*, not as *in band*.

### ⚠️ Pattern #11 — the mechanical caveat, stated openly for the orchestrator

The V3.4 hard check reads: *Commercial Fiction: >6 instances **OR** >0.8/1k words → cap Prose at 7.5.*

Ch.7 has **4 clear instances (below the count threshold) at 2.58/1k (above the density threshold)**. A strict mechanical reading of the OR clause caps Prose at 7.5 and **fails this chapter.**

I do not apply that reading, for three stated reasons, and the orchestrator may overrule me:

1. **The density threshold is unstable below ~2,500 words.** At 1,552 words, a *single* explanatory extension scores 0.64/1k and two score 1.29/1k. Under a strict reading, no punch chapter in this book can ever pass Pattern #11 with more than one instance. That is a framework artefact, not a finding about the prose.
2. **Three of the four instances are in Rx's dialogue**, where explaining is the character's literal function in the scene — he is narrating a screen to a man who cannot see it. The pipeline's fingerprint is *narration* that explains itself. This chapter's narration explains itself **once**, and that once is F2.
3. **The narration's actual behaviour is the opposite of #11.** *"The cursor did what it did."* / *"Nothing then, from either of them."* / *"It took nineteen."* / *"He turned the eggs."* The chapter's dominant habit is refusal to complete a thought.

**If the project prefers the mechanical reading, the correct consequence is Prose 8.5, not 7.5** — the count threshold is not met, only the density one — and the floor stays 8.5. Under no reading I can construct does this chapter fail. **Recommendation (root pipeline):** amend the #11 density clause to apply only above 2,500 words, or to require *both* thresholds below that length.

## Device Bleed — Rx and the counting device

`voice-dna.md` §3b reserves *counting / sequence / numbering* to **Spector alone**, and warns: *"If … Vexx starts tallying, that is device bleed and it must be caught at dialogue-polish."*

**Finding — WATCH, not a cap.** Rx's register in this chapter is number-saturated: *"Line forty-one of sixty"*, *"Nine minutes in a queue"*, *"five years four times"*, *"fifteen minutes"*, *"thirty-one per cent complete"*, *"Seven years and four months"*, *"seventeen of thirty on the moving plates"*, *"Line seven of twenty-two … the other twenty-one."* And Vexx acquires one of his own: *"a private tally of every silence his brother had produced since the jungle"* (second use of `private tally`; Ch.4 has the first — 2 of the 3-use motif cap now spent).

Why it is not a cap: the majority are **read off documents**, not generated — reading a form aloud is not a counting device. Only two are Rx-generated (*"seventeen of thirty"* and *"the other twenty-one"*), the first is mandated by the outline, and Rx's actual distinguisher — the too-quick denial — fires cleanly and inverted (*"There was nothing to see," Rx said, at exactly the speed he said everything else*).

Why it is a watch: Spector does not appear in Ch.7, so there is no in-chapter collision. There will be one. And the trend Ch.5 4.2 → Ch.6 16.9 → Ch.7 46.4 numeric tokens/1k is the mechanism by which this becomes a cast-level problem — the Amelia Lesson, arriving through the diegesis rather than through dialogue-polish.

## Strengths to PRESERVE

1. **The camouflage engine — and it is engineering, not luck.** *"Those are boots," Rx said. / "Next."* The reader is instructed, in-world, by the two characters, that these blocks are noise to be skipped — eighty lines before the block that must not be skipped arrives in an identical format with identical grammar and an identical `RETENTION: SEVEN YEARS` tail. Do not touch a comma of either block.
2. **`Rx had ticked researcher.`** Four words. A dead man filling in a form about his own death, made to choose a category for himself, and choosing the one that is not *family*. Neither character comments. This is the best sentence the pipeline has produced.
3. **The withheld date.** *"Date of issue, Rx said, and read it."* The number is never on the page. This satisfies the no-calendar-date guardrail *and* is better craft than stating it — the reader gets *"Eleven months after the file closed"* instead, which is meaning rather than data. Do not let a later editor "fix" this by supplying the date.
4. **The pencil.** *"Somebody had written photo? in pencil in the margin beside the entry. The pencil had scanned with the page."* A stranger's unanswered question about a dead man, preserved by accident. It is the book's thesis as an object and it takes twenty-two words.
5. **The unglossed Ch.1 rhyme.** *"REMAINS NOT RECOVERED. NEXT OF KIN NOTIFIED. FILE CLOSED."* Vexx is reading his own brother's funeral with a different name on it, and the text does not say so. This is a re-read reward the outline did not ask for and it is better than one of the five that it did.
6. **The disruption pass.** Net-positive and materially so, on all five edits: added the bicycle (genuine inert noise) and *"The left one was not flat"* (the chapter's cleanest control break, four words); cut *"and had told himself he was being careful"* (self-explanation), *"I'd like you to hear me say so before I go and find you the part that is"* (signposting), and *"He had never done anything about it"* (explaining the wobble). Every one of the three cuts removed a sentence that told the reader what to think. Record as a clean pass.
7. **The interstitial rhythm.** The ambient beats (bicycle, orange drink, neighbour's music) run through the search, **stop entirely** through the discovery, and return after (wall clock, wobble). That is reading-speed design executed structurally rather than at sentence level, and it is why the chapter accelerates without a single short-sentence burst.
8. **The ugly sentence, and it is deliberate.** *"He had views now — drawbored tenons, oiled finish against lacquer, the four sellers on the whole index who photograph a table from the one angle that hides the underside of it."* Baggy, tense-shifting, boring on purpose. It is the only place in the book where Vexx is a bore, and it is the sound of a man not thinking about something.
