### 1 — Simile Surgery: the shopping list

The comparison is kept; the **explanation of the comparison** is cut. `like items on a shopping
list` already delivers the flatness, the sameness of weight, the absence of judgement. The
thirteen-word tail then restated it in the abstract — *standing in her book afterward exactly
as they had come out of him* — which is the pipeline's signature move: showing, then unpacking
what was shown. The paragraph's own earlier clauses (*drawing no line between them, not looking
up while she did it*) had already proved the point twice. Reader supplies the rest.

**No comparison was removed.** The chapter's comparison rate is unchanged at **2.2/1k** against
its floor of 2.0 — the brief's "room to cut roughly one" was not spent, because it did not need
to be. Cutting an extension is free; cutting a bare comparison would have been tidying.

The sentence goes 57w → 44w and **stays inside the ≥40w bucket**, so the metric the brief warns
about does not move at all (17/115 before and after).

### 2 — the same operation with no simile in it

`which is a thing a man cannot do twice in front of another man without it being noticed` is the
narrator explaining the social mechanism of a gesture — and then the very next sentence
*demonstrates* it: **Jameson turned toward him and gave him his whole attention, and waited.**
The gloss tells the reader what is about to be shown one clause later. Cutting it leaves three
flat beats (`He had his mouth open. He shut it. Then he did it again.`) running straight into
the man noticing, which is both faster and colder.

This was also the chapter's **only `, which` narration gloss**: 0.41/1k → **0.00**.

### 3 — Emotional Control Break: the thank-you he was told not to say

The brief pointed here and it was right. The management that held and should not is **“It’ll
keep.”** — a clean, composed, service-register deflection, arriving about four seconds after a
sentence about his brother failed to arrive at all. He recovers too well.

Now it does not recover. What comes out in place of the sentence he could not finish is the
thing he has already said once and has already been asked not to say:

> “My brother’s—”
>
> Three words, and then whatever came after them did not arrive.
>
> […]
>
> “It’ll keep,” Vexx said. “Thank you.”
>
> “As you like.”

Two words, no narration, no reflection, nobody remarks on it. Jameson's *“Don’t thank me. I read
a disposition line. That isn’t a favor, it’s Thursday”* is twelve lines above it, and this is the
third time in the chapter Vexx thanks somebody with more in it than he meant to put there — the
first to Dessen (*"more in it than he had meant to put there"*), the second to Jameson in form,
this one out of control, and the fourth is the barometer at the close, which nobody hears.

**It is the chapter's stated emotional surprise executed as a leak instead of as a description**:
"genuinely grateful… and being unable to stop." Costs nothing structurally; touches no narration
metric; adds no `and`, no em-dash, no question mark.

### 4 — The Missing Paragraph, executed as a partial deletion

The whole-paragraph deletion the spec asks for was **considered and rejected on measurement**
(see *Operations NOT Applied*). What was deleted instead is the most predictable clause in the
chapter — the one you can write before you read it:

> He let it go, entirely, **the way a decent man does** — and stood there with him…

That is (a) the narrator grading a character the reader has just watched behave, (b) a near-
verbatim lift of the outline's own beat language (*"then lets it go, because that is what a
decent man does"*), and (c) the third consecutive rendering of the same idea in eleven lines —
*Jameson… waited* (l.292), the 65-word paragraph of him waiting (l.298), then this. Two of those
are observed behaviour. The third was a verdict.

The behaviour stays entirely intact: *He did not ask again. He did not tilt his head, or say* are
you sure*, or leave a door propped open in his voice… He stood there with him, in a corridor, in
front of an elevator that was not coming, saying nothing.* The reader supplies "decent," which is
the one word in this chapter the reader most needs to arrive at unassisted.

It also removes the chapter's only `the way [x]` construction (1 → 0, cap ≤2).

## Strongest Disruption

**Operation 3 — “Thank you.”**

Two words, and they are the chapter thesis said out loud by the wrong person at the wrong moment.
The scene's whole engine is that Dessen is right, Vexx did it, and the man who saved him is the
man who killed his brother — and the reader's discomfort depends entirely on Vexx's gratitude
being *involuntary*. Before this edit, the coda's last emotional event was a competent man making
a competent deflection. Now the deflection is followed, immediately, by the thing leaking out
through the gap the deflection left. Nothing explains it. Nobody notices it. Jameson's *"As you
like"* now answers two sentences instead of one and is no more knowing for it.

Operation 4 is a close second, on a different axis: it is the only edit that removes the
narrator's opinion of Jameson from the page, and the chapter is measurably better for having no
opinion of him anywhere in it.

## The gate condition — my verdict after the pass

**It holds, and Operation 4 strengthens it.**

Only one of my four edits is anywhere near a Jameson line, and it is Operation 4, which **deletes
narration about him** rather than adding any. The removed clause — *He let it go, entirely, the
way a decent man does* — was the narrator vouching for him. Vouching is the adjacent failure to
menace: both are the prose telling the reader how to feel about a man the chapter's whole design
says the reader must be left alone with. What stands now is four consecutive observed
behaviours and no verdict.

Operation 3 puts two words in **Vexx's** mouth, not Jameson's. His existing reply, *"As you
like,"* is unchanged and was audited by the dialogue pass. I read it cold against the new line
asking the pass's own question — *can a reader who knows he is the murderer hear this as him
managing Vexx?* — and it does not survive the conversion attempt: it is a man declining to press
somebody who has just declined to speak, and it now happens to also wave off a thank-you he had
already waved off once. That is fondness and mild embarrassment. It is not permission, not
acceptance of a debt, and not a beat held for effect.

Operations 1 and 2 are in narration about Dessen's pen and Vexx's own mouth respectively.
Jameson is not in either sentence.

**No line of his was changed, restored or reworded. The dialogue pass's improvement — *"That much
in two days is a front"* — stands untouched.** He still arrives having read the file, wins on a
disposition line, apologises for four specific things, worries about his own standing with a
woman he just met, and leaves mid-sentence about an aneroid barometer with the doors taking the
rest of it.

**Dessen.** Her case is untouched. Not one word of her finding, her questions, her ruling or her
transfer was edited. Operation 1 cut thirteen words of *narration about her handwriting* and
nothing else. She remains correct, unhurried, not hostile, not a stooge, and still right at the
end — the recommendation falls away on a fact she missed, not on rank. The protected impulse
passage (*"You didn't destroy it. You did something narrower. You made it useless to him."*) is
**byte-identical**, as is the entire scene break containing it.
