## 2. The missing paragraph — why it is a sentence

**There is no deletable paragraph in this chapter, and I looked for one properly.** Every
candidate is either protected or load-bearing: ¶30 is Echo's punchline, ¶152 is the admission
that is not an admission, ¶158 is the 70/30 adjudication, ¶166 is the one texture beat the
chapter already had, ¶200–202 are the just-installed N-11 fix, ¶210 is the repaired coda, and
¶144 is the chapter's ugly sentence. That is a well-built chapter and I am reporting it as one.

**What there was, was one sentence doing the pipeline's signature move** — the reflective gloss
laid on top of a good image:

```
BEFORE  The sentence came out whole, and he had not built it standing there — he had been
        carrying it for weeks, cut down and cut down again, the size of a thing you could
        hand across a corridor. Until it was out of him he had not understood what he had
        been trimming it for.

AFTER   The sentence came out whole, and he had not built it standing there — he had been
        carrying it for weeks, cut down and cut down again, the size of a thing you could
        hand across a corridor.
```

Apply the operation's own test: does the extension add meaning the reader cannot infer, or does
it restate what the image showed? ***"the size of a thing you could hand across a corridor"*
already names the purpose — literally, in the image, including the corridor he is standing in.**
The next sentence then reports him realising what the image has just told the reader. It is a
restatement wearing the clothes of a turn. Cut, and *"Which one."* now lands on the reveal with
nothing between them.

**Checked before cutting, per the Devoted Reader layer:** this is **not** one of the five
re-read rewards in `foundation.md` §4f (Ch.1→21, Ch.2→15, Ch.7→15, Ch.3→15, Ch.13→14), and it
plants nothing. **The outline's writer-warning (b) is intact and strengthened:** the relief has
to be *visible and unflattering*, and it is carried by *"cut down and cut down again"* — a man
who has spent weeks manufacturing a minimum — which is more unflattering unglossed, because
nobody is now apologising for it on his behalf. Metric-neutral in every direction but one: 17
words, **no em-dash, no adverb, no `and`**, and not one of the six long sentences.

## 3. ¶186 — the last unactioned narration finding in the chapter

Both prior passes logged it and neither could action it: *"turning it over"* is **Vexx's**
deliberation verb, author-established four times and **named as his in his own prose** at Ch.2
¶253 (*"the way he turned over everything eventually"*), and Ch.11 ¶186 had handed it to Gaia.
That is **instance two of a second user** — precisely where the tidying gesture stood before it
reached ten instances across five characters, and `character-bible.md`'s one-device map now
registers it with that warning attached.

```
BEFORE  She stood there turning it over in front of him without hurrying — he watched her
        find the edges of it, find how small it was, not say so.

AFTER   She stood there listening to it in front of him without hurrying — he watched her
        find the edges of it, find how small it was, not say so.
```

**Three words for three words. Metric-neutral:** no em-dash change, no adverb change, no `and`
change, no word-count change, repeated-phrases gate still clean.

**And it is an upgrade, not a swap.** The object-handling conceit downstream (*find the edges of
it, find how small it was*) did not have to go with it — **it works better**, because finding
the edges of a room and finding how small it is **is what Gaia does for a living**, and is what
the chapter spent two thousand words teaching the reader to watch her do. She listened to the
corridor and found it dead; she now listens to his one sentence and finds out how small the room
is. **The chapter's own system, turned on the thing he just handed her** — the same move the
hook pass made with the closer. Her device, on her line, and Vexx's returned to him.

**Not reintroduced:** nothing negate-then-substitute, no count, no restatement. `grep -ci
"turning it over"` on Ch.11 → **0**, including the header comment, which I reworded so that a
future word-level audit does not re-find the bleed in a note describing its removal.

   notice about a culprit, which rhymed harder and was comic in the wrong place). It is also
   deliberately **not a noise** — in a chapter that teaches the reader to hear rooms, any added
