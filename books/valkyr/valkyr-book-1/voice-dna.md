# Voice DNA — Valkyr: Book One

> **This is the executable spec.** `foundation.md` §6 *defines* the voice; this file makes it
> enforceable. The Writer writes FROM it; Dialogue Polish and Hook Craft check AGAINST it; the
> Disruptor cuts to its anti-pattern budget; the Editor and Evaluator hold the line with it.
>
> **Everything in §1 is reverse-engineered from the author's own prose** in
> `research/original-draft.md`, with quoted evidence and measured densities. This is not a
> voice being invented. It is a voice being *described accurately enough to continue*.

## 1. GLOBAL NARRATIVE VOICE

*How the book sounds when nobody is speaking.*

### 1.1 The parameters

**Vocabulary level: 6/10.** Plain Anglo-Saxon nouns and verbs, used with unusual precision,
with a deliberate reach roughly once a page.
> *"He was a very expensive rumor of one."*

**Sentence rhythm: BIMODAL — flat short declaratives against long accumulating retrospection.**
The long sentences add clause after clause and put the emotion at the very end.
> *"He remembered walking out of that building for the last time in a uniform that already
> felt like it belonged to someone else, the base noise around him — dropship engines
> spooling, drill instructors barking somewhere out past the motor pool, the particular
> clatter of a mess hall at 1100 — receding behind him like a tide going out."*

And then, immediately after:
> *"He'd expected relief. He got something closer to vertigo."*

**Formality: measured.** Professional-military baseline with a private, near-confessional
undertow. Institutional vocabulary is used *correctly and without irony by the narration*; the
irony is manufactured entirely in the reader.

**Humor: dry, sparse, load-bearing, and almost entirely Rx's.** Its function is to prove two
men share a private language.
> *"Excuses. I died and I'm still a better shot than you."*

**Emotional register: restrained, tending to numb.** Feeling is displaced onto objects and
procedure. The draft reports a physical fact and lets the reader do the arithmetic.
> *"Vexx's knees very nearly went out from under him. He put a hand against the doorframe to
> keep upright."*

### 1.2 The four distinctive features (with evidence)

**(a) The retrospective narrator who knows more than the scene.** The book's defining move —
and its most dangerous, because it is one degree from an analytical simile.
> *"He had no way of knowing, sitting in that chair, that the man across the table had watched
> his brother die at close range and personally written the report that buried it."*
>
> **Budget: ≤2 per chapter, and NEVER in a chapter opening.** Cap 3 uses of the explicit
> *"he would remember this later"* formulation across the whole book.

**(b) Naming the technique instead of the feeling.** The narration explains *why people behave
as they do professionally*, never what they feel.
> *"it was armor, the only way to say a sentence like that twice a week and keep functioning
> as a human being afterward."*

**(c) The unquoted interface voice.** Rx and the five AIs speak without quotation marks
(italic in the author's original). This makes the most intimate presence in the book
typographically *not in the room*, which is the single best formal decision in the draft.
**Adopt it as a convention and hold it manuscript-wide.**

**(d) Deliberate flatness at maximum load.** The five-word paragraph at the highest-pressure
moment in Chapter One:
> *"They buried an empty casket."*

### 1.3 VOICE UNDER PRESSURE (mandatory — the voice must break)

**Primary break: THE PROSE GOES FLAT.** Not fragmented, not lyrical — *underdescriptive.*
Sentences shorten; the long accumulating mode stops entirely; interiority switches off; the
narration reports physical facts in sequence like a log entry. This is the author's own move
and it is the voice's best weapon.

**Four pressure behaviours, all drawn from the draft:**

1. **Present-tense leakage.** Past-tense narration slips into present for a sentence or two
   at maximum load and slips back with no comment.
   > *"Grief, he learned, isn't a wound. It's a phantom limb. The nerve endings don't know the
   > arm is gone."*

2. **The unanswered question, WITHOUT QUESTION MARKS.** The author's own punctuation choice
   and a genuine signature. Preserve it exactly.
   > *"Why authorize a transfer in the field. Why preserve one soldier's mind out of however
   > many hadn't made it home that year. Why him."*

3. **The catalogue.** Physical facts listed in place of a feeling. *A scorched fragment of
   armor plating. A half-melted data chip. A single dented dog tag.*

4. **Direct-address collapse.** At the very worst moments the narration slides into Vexx's
   own unmarked interior first person and back out again.
   > *"there was never going to be anything in there. There's nothing to bury. There's just a
   > box shaped like an apology, and I'm standing here accepting it because there's nothing
   > else on offer."*

**Where the voice must break in the new chapters:** Ch.13 (the collapse structure — the whole
chapter), Ch.14 (flatness plus one laugh), Ch.20 (flat, then present-tense leakage twice),
Ch.21 (flat throughout, maximum — no interiority at all for 2,000 words).

### 1.4 ANTI-PATTERNS — what this voice NEVER does

- **Never reports an emotional temperature.** Zero instances in 14,776 words. Not one wave of
  anything washing over anyone.
- **Never explains psychology in a simile.** The draft's similes are physical and behavioural
  (*"like a tide going out"*), never analytical.
- **Never uses metacognitive narration.** No *"he realised he was building a case."*
- **Never puts an adverb in a dialogue tag.**
- **Never lets Jameson be sinister on the page.** (Gate condition.)
- **Never enters Rx.** Everything about him is inference from a processed voice and a cursor.
- **Never uses "as if" / "as though."** Once in 14,776 words. Treat as effectively banned.
- **Never opens a chapter with weather-as-mood, waking, arriving, or a rhetorical question.**

## 2. PER-CHARACTER VOICE CARDS

**These are the source of `character-bible.md`.** Full cards with DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEES live
there; this section is the differentiation logic behind them. Every card in the bible is built
on the four axes — **attention/worldview, syntax, evasion, physicality/silence** — with a
verbal tic present on only three cards in the entire book.

Summary of the axes as applied to this cast:

| Character | Attention / worldview | Syntax fingerprint | Evasion — never says | Physicality / silence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Vexx** | Reduces the world to *next actions*; sees procedure where others see feeling | Bimodal — clipped in scene, accumulating in narration | Never names his own emotion; never says "I'm afraid" | Goes still and answers a beat late |
| **Rx** | Reduces the world to *his brother's condition*; monitors Vexx constantly | Joke-first, meaning-second; demotes tense | Never says "how I died"; never finishes the sentence about the rain | The cursor's rhythm; the half-syllable catch |
| **Jameson** | Reduces the world to *outcomes and decisions*; sees indecision as the only vice | Concrete nouns, precise numbers, pre-empts your objection | Never euphemises; never says "unfortunate" or "tragic" | Stands when you enter; never raises his voice |
| **Aglaope** | Reduces the world to *who in this room is carrying something* | Statements with a long pause built in | Never says "sorry"; never says "it's okay" | Reaches over and rests a hand on an arm |
| **Goliath** | Reduces the world to *the job and whether it was done honestly* | Declaratives, concrete nouns, no abstractions | Never speaks in abstractions; never says a feeling word | Asks one question and waits too long |
| **Gaia** | Reduces the world to *sightlines, exits and acoustics* | Fastest; fact → ask → clock; no adjectives | Never asks for reassurance; never says "I trust you" | Arms crossed, chair angled at the door |
| **Paladin** | Reduces the world to *who might get left* | Restates your position, then disagrees; "we" not "I" | Never names the person he couldn't carry | "No." As a complete sentence |
| **Zeus** | Reduces the world to *what a person is protecting* | Questions in the wrong order; second-person conditional | Never asks a personal question uninvited; never comforts | Sits apart; stays seated when others stand |
| **Voss** | Reduces the world to *what has to be processed today* | Clipped imperatives; almost no modal verbs | Never explains herself; never says "should" | Returns everything face-down |
| **Corwin** | Reduces the world to *whether he is believed* → later, *whether he is being agreeable* | Precise → hesitant → agreeing | Never stops asking about his daughter | Grammar correction (Ch.3, Ch.6) — then its absence (Ch.19) |
| **Dessen** | Reduces the world to *chain of custody* | Same question three times, one word changed | Never speculates; never says "I think" | The paper notebook, and the pen |
| **Spector** | Reduces the world to *sequence and craft* | Shortest sentences in the book; no filler | Never says what Phantom was to him | The countdown that isn't needed |
| **Echo** | Reduces the world to *whatever is currently happening, out loud* | Run-ons, present tense, mid-sentence entrances | Never stops talking; never lets a silence stand | Transmitting when he shouldn't |
| **Bastion** | Reduces the world to *bodies and their condition* | Question-first; body-check before content | Never says "casualty" | The unasked-for medical question |
| **Hollow** | Reduces the world to *what is worth saying yet* | Precise, unhurried, withholding | Never softens a finding | Speaks least; answers forty minutes late |
| **Requiem** | Reduces the world to *who is unfinished* | Softest; the singing image, three times only | Never says she is good at the against-part | The pause before she agrees |

## 3. VOICE DIFFERENTIATION MATRIX (the cover-the-name test, pre-solved)

| Character | Sharpest distinguisher | Contrast vs nearest character |
|---|---|---|
| **Vexx** | Answers a question by **correcting its vocabulary** | vs **Zeus**, who reframes the *situation* rather than the *word*. Vexx edits terms; Zeus edits premises. |
| **Rx** | **The too-quick denial**, arriving a beat before an honest answer could | vs **Aglaope**, who also under-answers — but she *pauses* first and Rx never does. Silence vs speed. |
| **Jameson** | **Pre-empts your objection and states it better than you would**, then answers it | vs **Zeus**, who also anticipates — but Zeus tells you what you're *doing*; Jameson tells you what you're *thinking*, and agrees with it. |
| **Aglaope** | **Names what she has noticed without asking permission** | vs **Gaia**, who also states things flatly — but Gaia states *facts about the world* and Aglaope states *facts about you*. |
| **Goliath** | **Trade vocabulary for moral positions** ("clearing a threat vs clearing a problem") | vs **Paladin**, the other moral voice — who leads with *regulation* and arrives at ethics. Goliath leads with *craft*. |
| **Gaia** | **Fact → ask → clock**, in that order, with "your call" as a weapon | vs **Voss**, also clipped — but Voss gives a fact and *stops*. Gaia gives a fact and demands a decision. |
| **Paladin** | **Restates your position accurately before disagreeing**, then "No." as a sentence | vs **Goliath** (above) and vs **Bastion**, who also leads with others' welfare — but Bastion asks about *bodies*, Paladin about *positions*. |
| **Zeus** | **Questions in a deliberately wrong order**; never asks why | vs **Dessen**, who also questions methodically — but Dessen repeats the *same* question, and Zeus never repeats anything. |
| **Voss** | **Fact, then silence.** Almost no modal verbs. | vs **Hollow**, the other withholder — but Hollow withholds until it is *useful*, and Voss withholds because explaining is effort. |
| **Corwin** | The **grammar correction**, and then its conspicuous absence | vs everyone: he is the only character whose distinguisher is *removed* across the book. |
| **Dessen** | **The same question three times, one word changed** | vs **Zeus** (above). Repetition vs sequence. |
| **Spector** | **Sequence/countdown**; the shortest sentences of any voice | vs **Bastion**, the other service-cadence AI — Bastion counts nothing and asks about hands. |
| **Echo** | **Never lets a silence stand**; names things and uses the names as if agreed | vs **Requiem**, the other warm AI — Requiem's entire technique is the pause. |
| **Bastion** | **Body-check question before content**; will not say "casualty" | vs **Spector** (above) and vs **Aglaope** — Aglaope notices what you're *carrying*, Bastion asks whether your *hand* is stiff. |
| **Hollow** | **Answers a question nobody asked, forty minutes late** | vs **Voss** (above). |
| **Requiem** | **The pause before agreement**, and the singing image (3× max) | vs **Echo** (above) and vs **Aglaope**, her own operator — Aglaope reaches, Requiem waits. |

**No two characters share a sharpest distinguisher.** The tightest pairs are Vexx/Zeus

and again in `character-bible.md`.

## 3b. TIC BUDGET — the anti-"everyone has a catchphrase" rule (HARD)

**Distinctness is NOT "give everyone a verbal tic."** A cast where everyone has a catchphrase
is the single most common way a large ensemble collapses into one voice wearing different
hats. This book has **sixteen speaking voices**, which puts it squarely in the danger zone.
The budget is designed up front and is a **hard ceiling**.

### Tic-bearer roster — THREE characters, whole book

| Tic-bearer | Device | Why it is EARNED |
|---|---|---|
| **RX** | **The too-quick denial** — *"Fine." / "I'm fine, Vexx."* arriving a beat faster than an honest answer | It is not a speech habit. **It is the seal talking.** The suppression protocol producing a reflex the man himself does not author. It is the book's central plot mechanism wearing the shape of a verbal tic, and it must *break* in Ch.13 and never be smooth again. |
| **ECHO** | **Compulsive narration — never letting a silence stand** | His donor was a scout-sniper who died alone behind enemy lines, going quiet. Series-canon-mandated compulsion, not decoration. |
| **SPECTOR** | **Sequence / the unnecessary countdown** (*set-charge-clear*) | Phantom's craft, surviving the transfer. Already established in the author's drafted Ch.2 and load-bearing for the Phantom reveal. |

**Everyone else in this book has NO verbal tic** and is distinguished by
worldview / syntax / evasion / physicality. That includes **Jameson**, deliberately: a
catchphrase would make him legible, and legibility is a gate failure.

### One-device-per-character map (no two characters share a device)

| Device | Owner — and NOBODY else |
|---|---|
| Counting ALOUD / sequence / countdown / numbering — counting as craft, performed for a room | **Spector** |
| The private, unspoken tally — kept for nobody, never said out loud | **Vexx** (and Rx keeping one *for* Vexx). See `character-bible.md` §THE ONE CARVE-OUT. |
| Continuous narration / filling silence | **Echo** |
| Too-quick denial / reflex deflection | **Rx** |
| Body-check questions ("is that hand still stiff") | **Bastion** — a *question habit*, never numeric |
| Naming/labelling things and using the name later | **Echo** |
| Restating the other's position before disagreeing | **Paladin** |
| Correcting the terms of a question | **Vexx** — **CAPPED, see below** |
| Pre-empting the objection and improving it | **Jameson** |
| Reframing the situation | **Zeus** |
| Withholding until useful / answering late | **Hollow** |
| Fact-then-silence, no modals | **Voss** |
| Repeating one question with a word changed | **Dessen** |
| Naming what she notices, unasked | **Aglaope** |
| Fact → ask → clock | **Gaia** |
| Trade vocabulary for ethics | **Goliath** |
| The pause before agreement | **Requiem** |
| The singing image | **Requiem** — max 3 uses, whole book |
| Grammar correction | **Corwin** — and its removal is the point |

> **Counting, listing, pricing, rating, quantifying and cataloguing are the SAME DEVICE in
> different coats**, and **Spector owns the SPOKEN form.** If Bastion starts keeping a headcount,
> or Gaia starts rating things numerically, **that is device bleed and
> it must be caught at dialogue-polish, not at chapter twenty.**

### CAPPED (declared, not exempt — the ALLOWLIST is a capped registry)

| Item | Cap (whole book) | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Vexx's term-correction ("I'm careful." / "There's a difference.") | **5 uses** | 2 spent in the draft. It is his signature and it calcifies fast. |
| Rx's literal *"I'm fine"* / *"Fine."* | **5 uses** | 3 spent in the draft. The *behaviour* may recur more often; the **phrase** may not. |
| Rx calling Vexx **"brother"** | **5 uses** | 3 spent in the draft. It is a weight, not an address. |
| Requiem's singing image | **3 uses** | 1 spent (drafted Ch.2). Final use is Ch.16. |
| RECLAMATION (the word) | **9 uses** | Its power is scarcity. |
| *"shaped like an apology"* | **2 uses total** | 1 spent (Ch.1). The second and last is Ch.26. |
| *"he would remember this later"* formulation | **3 uses**, none in a chapter opening | The author has earned the move; the Writer has not. |
| Retrospective-narrator intrusions | **≤2 per chapter** | Never in an opening. |

### RETIRED / AT-RISK PHRASES

`voice_wear_check.py` and the Writer must both watch these. The first four are **CLOSED for
new chapters** — they are at or over cap in the drafted five.

| Phrase / construction | Draft uses | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **`filed it away` / `filed away`** | **7** | **CLOSED.** No new chapter may use it. The motif *transforms* into Corwin's Ch.19 line, *"They don't kill you. They file you."* |
| **`not yet a problem`** | **3** | **CLOSED.** At cap. |
| **`a shape on the horizon`** | **2** | **CLOSED.** |
| **`the vocabulary to name it`** | **2** | **CLOSED.** |
| **`the particular ___`** | **9 (0.61/1k)** | **The draft's most calcified habit.** Ceiling for new chapters: **0.25 / 1,000 words** (≈1 per 4,000-word chapter). Do not sand it out of Ch.1–5. |
| `never once` | 1 | **RETIRED — series-level.** THE AMELIA LESSON (below). Do not use again in this book. |
| `too quick` (as narration of Rx's denial) | 2 | At risk. Vary the verb; the *behaviour* is the tic, not the description of it. |
| `the way X does Y` construction | 18 (1.22/1k) | At risk. Monitor; do not exceed 1.5/1k. |

> **THE AMELIA LESSON.** In an earlier book on this pipeline, a quantifying/counting device
> and the phrase *"never once"* calcified into a tic **and bled across multiple characters**
> until the cast started sounding alike, and a manuscript-wide de-tic pass was needed to fix
> it. This book is structurally at risk in the same way: sixteen speaking voices, six of them
> AIs with service-cadence registers that want to converge. **Prevention by construction:** the
> roster above (3 tic-bearers), the one-device map (no sharing, and counting belongs to Spector
> alone), and the capped registry. **The specific bleed to watch here is Vexx's term-correction
> device migrating onto Zeus or Gaia, and Spector's counting migrating onto Bastion.**
>
> **The watch-list was wrong, and here is what the migration actually did.** Through Ch.6-8 the
> predicted routes stayed clean — nothing went to Bastion, and Zeus/Paladin and Gaia/Aglaope,
> the pairs everyone was watching, held. The bleed went where nobody was looking:
>
> - **Goliath** picked up **Vexx's term-correction shape**, twice.
> - **Gaia** applied **Spector's reserved *N-of-the-M* construction** — to people's interior
>   states, which is worse than to objects.
> - **Zeus** asked **why**, which he does nowhere else in the book, announced he would repeat a
>   question (Dessen's axis), and used Rx's Ch.7 witness-request shape and Goliath's trade
>   register. Six edits on one character.
> - **Paladin** spoke **Rx's capped denial** (*"It's fine."*), and **Goliath** spoke
>   **Spector's countdown** verbatim.
>
> The lesson is not a better list of pairs. It is that **a predicted watch-list makes you blind
> to the routes it does not name.** Audit every speaking character against every reserved
> device, not the two you expect to collide — and note that every line above passed the
> Cover-the-Name Test, because it measures whether a line is DISTINCTIVE, never whose it is.

## 4. ANTI-PATTERN BUDGET (genre-adjusted, per 1,000 words)

Genre: military SF / conspiracy thriller with literary interiority. The band would normally
allow ≤6 similes/1k; **this book is set tighter, at the literary end, because the author's own
draft measures 3.38 and the voice's power comes from restraint.**

| Pattern | Ceiling (new chapters) | Draft baseline | Enforcement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Explanatory / analytical similes (#11 — the #1 AI fingerprint)** | **≤ 3.5 / 1k**, and **zero** that explain psychology | 3.38 / 1k, all physical or behavioural | Disruptor cuts to ceiling. **An explanatory simile is a defect at any density.** |
| **Adverbs in dialogue tags** | **0** | 0 | Zero tolerance |
| **"as if" / "as though"** | **≤ 0.15 / 1k** (≈1 per 6,000 words) | 0.07 / 1k | Effectively banned |
| **Metacognitive narration** | **0** | 0 | Zero tolerance |
| **Emotional temperature reports** | **0** | 0 | Zero tolerance — the hardest rule in the book |
| **Em-dashes** | **~8 / 1k in new chapters** (book-level gate ~10) | **10.35 / 1k** | The drafted chapters run at the ceiling. New chapters aim lower to leave the *book-level* gate headroom. **Do not sand Ch.1–5.** |
| **"the particular ___"** | **≤ 0.25 / 1k** | 0.61 / 1k | Author signature, over-used; ration it |
| **Retrospective-narrator intrusion** | **≤ 2 per chapter**, never in an opening | ~2–3/ch in Ch.1 | Ch.1 is locked and exempt |
| **Rhetorical questions in narration** | **≤ 1 per chapter**, and preferably as the author's no-question-mark form | Rare | — |
| **Sentence-length histogram** | **≥25 % of sentences ≤6 words AND ≥8 % ≥40 words** | 34.6 % / 11.2 % | **A fat middle is off-voice.** Check this before anything else. |
| **Dialogue share** | **28–40 %** combined (quoted + unquoted interface) | ~28–30 % | Ch.18 and Ch.25 run high; Ch.7, Ch.14 and Ch.21 run near zero. That spread is correct. |

**Additional book-specific prohibitions (gate conditions, not style preferences):**

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| Any rampancy vocabulary — timers, "how long does he have," fragmentation-as-countdown | STATE.yaml guardrail. The single easiest unearned tension to import from Halo canon. |
| Any sentence that knows Rx's interior state | POV lock |
| Any Jameson line readable as a threat, double meaning, or knowing aside | Gate condition |
| Any hard in-universe calendar date | STATE.yaml guardrail |
| Narration connecting *"file"* (Vexx's coping verb) to *"they file you"* (Ch.19) | The reader makes it. If the book makes it, the best line in Part Three dies. |
| Narration glossing RECLAMATION on its first appearance (Ch.7) | Kills the primary re-read reward |

## 6. QUICK CHECKLIST (pin this above the Writer's desk)

Before finalising any chapter:

1. Sentence histogram — is ≥25 % at ≤6 words and ≥8 % at ≥40? If the middle is fat, rewrite.
2. Zero emotional temperature reports. Zero metacognition. Zero explanatory similes.
3. Similes ≤3.5/1k. Em-dashes ~8/1k. "the particular" ≤0.25/1k.
4. Did any character acquire a tic who is not Rx, Echo or Spector? Cut it.
5. Did counting/listing/rating appear in anyone but Spector? Cut it.
6. Is `filed it away`, `not yet a problem`, `a shape on the horizon`, `the vocabulary to name
   it` or `never once` anywhere in the chapter? Remove it.
7. Does any sentence know what Rx feels? Rewrite it as inference.
8. Is there a Jameson line that is cleverer for the reader than for Vexx? Delete it.
9. Cover the names on every line of dialogue. Can you still tell? If not, open
   `character-bible.md`.
10. Did a new named character appear? **Add their card to `character-bible.md` before
    finalising**, and re-check the tic budget.
