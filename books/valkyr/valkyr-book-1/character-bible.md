## How distinctness works here (read before adding a character)

Distinctness is **NOT** "give everyone a verbal tic." A cast where every character has a
catchphrase reads as machine-made — it is the most common way a large cast collapses into one
voice wearing hats. **This book has sixteen speaking voices, six of them AIs sharing a
service-cadence register.** It is squarely in the danger zone, and the four axes are what keep
it out:

1. **Attention / worldview** — what they NOTICE and reduce the world to (the deepest
   differentiator by a distance).
2. **Syntax / register** — sentence shape and formality (clips vs spirals; subordinates vs
   fragments; questions vs statements).
3. **Evasion** — what they will NOT say. Silence characterizes harder than speech.
4. **Physicality / silence** — the body-tell or pause that stands in for a spoken tic.

A verbal tic is a fifth thing, **rare and earned**. Seventeen of the twenty characters below
carry **none**.

### Book-specific hard constraints that shape every card

- **POV is Vexx, close third, whole book.** No Jameson POV. No Rx POV. Every card below
  describes what is **observable from outside** — because that is all the narration is
  permitted to have. A card that describes an interior state describes something the Writer may
  infer and dramatise, never assert.
- **Rx is only ever a processed voice and a cursor.** His "physicality" is timing, artifacting
  and rhythm. He has no body and the prose must never lend him one.
- **Jameson is institutional, never cartoonish.** He is warm, credible and genuinely
  persuasive. **He has no verbal tic on purpose** — a catchphrase would make him legible, and a
  scene where the reader can see he is the villain is a failed scene.
- **No rampancy vocabulary anywhere**, from any AI, in any register. No timers, no "how long
  do I have," no fragmentation-as-countdown.
- **Naming and spelling obey `../SERIES-BIBLE.md` exactly.** Vexxcerian/Vexx · Rx · Richard
  Jameson · Goliath/Spector · Gaia/Echo · Paladin/Bastion · Zeus/Hollow · Aglaope/Requiem
  ("Lure" in program paperwork only) · Phantom · Corwin · Reyes · Voss · the Choosing ·
  Valkyr · SPARTAN-II · UNSC / ONI.
- **Dialogue convention (hold it manuscript-wide) — the register follows the NARRATION, not
  the speaker.** Quotation marks for speech in a **dramatized scene**; unquoted italics for
  speech reported inside **summary or retrospective narration**, *and* for the private
  interface channel (Rx and the five AIs, always). The author's own choice, and load-bearing
  twice over: it makes the most intimate presence in the book typographically *not in the
  room*, and it lets the prose move between scene and memory without announcing it.
  **The proof is Ch.1 — 58 italic runs and zero quotation marks, because the whole chapter is
  memory.** Quotation marks appear the moment Ch.2 begins dramatizing.
  **Consequence:** Voss speaks in italics in Ch.2 (summary) and in quotation marks in Ch.6
  (dramatized scene). That is the convention working, **not an inconsistency to normalise** —
  an evaluator pass read it as one and recommended "fixing" it. Do not.

> ### Logged bleed: "That's not X. That's Y"
>
> Flagged independently by the evaluator and by dialogue-polish. This two-beat corrective
> reframe is the **surface form of Zeus's device** (reframing rather than arguing), and it had
> spread to **Goliath** (Ch.3, the incendiary), **Zeus ×2**, and **Aglaope** — with Corwin
> acquiring eleven instances in the Ch.6 draft before they were cut to four.
>
> **Owner: ZEUS.** The instances already standing in the author's drafted Ch.1–5 are
> grandfathered — that is his prose and it is not being rewritten to satisfy a map. But **no
> NEW character may be given this shape**, and Corwin in particular is licensed for the grammar
> correction and nothing else. Catch it at dialogue-polish.
>
> ### WATCH (chapter-local so far): Rx's witness-requests
>
> Ch.7's draft gave Rx three witness-requests — *"I want that on the record"*, *"I want that
> understood"*, *"I'd like you to hear me say so"*. **The disruptor pass cut the third**, on
> the separate grounds that it pre-labelled the requisition as evidence; it now stands at two,
> which is the right number. The structure still pays off: the anchor lands precisely because
> the second reading arrives with **no** preface, so the habit exists to be withheld.
> **That is a chapter-local structure, not a licence.** Rx already holds his one device (the
> too-quick denial). If the witness-request shape appears again in Ch.8+, it becomes a second
> device for a tic-bearer who is capped at one — catch it at dialogue-polish.

> ### NOT bleed, do not "fix": Echo's approving hum
>
> Echo's *"small, approving hum"* in Ch.5 duplicates Rx's, and it is **deliberate** — the
> author's own narration says so on the page: *"mirrored, almost exactly, the sound Rx had made
> weeks earlier."* The doubling is the point. It is recorded here so a later pass does not
> read it as accidental and differentiate it away.

> **Counting, listing, pricing, rating, quantifying and cataloguing are the SAME DEVICE in
> different coats**, and **Spector owns the spoken form.** If Bastion starts a headcount, or
> Gaia starts rating things numerically, **that is device bleed and it must be caught at
> dialogue-polish, not at chapter twenty.**
>
> **THE ONE CARVE-OUT — the private tally, and the author made it, not the architect.** This
> rule originally read "only Spector may have any of them ... if Vexx starts tallying anything,
> that is device bleed," and it was wrong, because the author's own drafted Ch.4 does exactly
> that: *"Vexx had started, without quite admitting it to himself, keeping a private tally of
> which was which."* Three lines further down this same file, Rx's entry already assumed it.
> The rule contradicted the prose it was written to protect, and the prose wins.
>
> They are not the same device. **Spector counts OUT LOUD, at people, as craft** — the
> unnecessary countdown, *four drums, four seals, four intact.* It is competence performed for
> a room. **The private tally is counted for nobody**, never spoken, and its whole meaning is
> that the man keeping it will not admit he is keeping it. Rx's *"I keep it. Nobody's asked me
> to keep it. I don't know what I keep it for"* is the same device pointed back at Vexx, and it
> is the best line in Ch.7 precisely because it is private.
>
> So: **a spoken count from anyone but Spector is bleed. A silent one from Vexx or Rx is
> canon.** If a tally is ever said aloud to another character, it has crossed over.
>
> **The one near-collision, resolved:** Dessen's repetition is a *deliberate interrogation
> technique applied to questions*; Merrick's is an *involuntary echo of his own statements*.
> They are different devices and **they never share a scene** (Merrick is Ch.8 only; Dessen is
> Ch.10 only). If a later draft ever puts them in a room together, one of them loses the
> device.

## PRINCIPALS

#### VEXX (Vexxcerian) — POV, whole book; commander of the rebuilt cell
- **Attention / worldview:** Reduces the world to *next actions*. Handed a grief, he produces a
  procedure. He sees the shape of institutional language before he sees the person delivering
  it, and he reads rooms the way he was trained to read them, which is why he keeps missing
  what is in front of him.
- **Vocabulary band:** Thirty years of SPARTAN-II service and ONI paperwork. Plain
  Anglo-Saxon nouns and precise verbs, with military and clerical register leaking in
  constantly — *authorization*, *chain of custody*, *assessment*, *filed*. He uses exact verbs
  for inexact feelings.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **Bimodal.** Clipped declaratives in scene (median 12 words); long
  accumulating retrospective sentences in narration that add clause after clause and land the
  emotion at the very end. **No middle register.**
- **Evasion — never says:** His own emotion, by name. Never *"I'm afraid," "I'm angry," "I miss
  him."* Never asks for help. From Ch.17 onward, never says why he wrote a report the way he
  wrote it.
- **Physicality / silence:** He goes still and answers a beat late. Under maximum load he does
  not shake or shout — **he goes flat**, and reports physical facts in sequence like a log
  entry.
- **Verbal tic:** **None as a catchphrase.** He owns the *device* of correcting the terms of a
  question instead of answering it (*"I'm not angry. I'm careful."* / *"I suspect something.
  There's a difference."*), and **it is CAPPED at 5 uses across the book, 2 already spent.** It
  calcifies fast; ration it.
- **Chaos markers (full profile — see `foundation.md` §3):** the apartment he never unpacked and
  the furniture catalogues he reads at 0200 and never orders from (Ch.7, 15, 23) · retrospective
  determinism · the mess hall at 1100, mutating (Ch.6, 10, 21, 26) · snapping at a transit clerk
  about a seat assignment (Ch.19) and laughing alone at a death notice (Ch.14).
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** He is the only voice in the book that answers a question by
  **editing its vocabulary** — where Zeus edits the *premise* and Jameson edits your *objection*,
  Vexx edits the *word* — and the only one whose narration converts a feeling into a filing
  action.
- **Sample line:** *"I'm not hiding it. I'm not showing it. Those aren't the same thing."*

#### RX — Vexx's twin, running as an AI. **NEVER a POV.**
> Everything here is what **Vexx can observe or infer** from a processed voice and a cursor.
> The Writer may never assert Rx's interior state. If a sentence knows what Rx feels, it is a
> defect.
- **Attention / worldview:** Reduces the world to *his brother's condition.* He monitors Vexx
  constantly — sleep, marksmanship, whether he ate — and reports on everything except himself.
- **Vocabulary band:** Identical baseline to Vexx (same childhood, same program, same
  augmentation windows) with four years of diagnostic vocabulary layered over it. **He demotes
  his own experience into system language:** terror becomes *an artifact*, an unexplained memory
  becomes *a processing hiccup*.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **Joke first, meaning second** — and the meaning is the actual
  message. He grammatically demotes his own tense: *"I was. Am. I don't actually know which
  tense applies anymore, brother."*
- **Evasion — never says:** How he died. What is behind the door. He also never finishes the
  sentence about the rain on the barracks roof — he tries three times across the book, gets it
  wrong differently each time, and gives up (Ch.20).
- **Physicality / silence:** **He has no body. His body is timing.** The cursor's rhythm,
  which reads "less like machinery and more like breathing." The half-syllable catch on certain
  names. Two distinguishable kinds of silence — the processing kind and the other kind — and
  Vexx has started privately tallying which is which.
- **Verbal tic:** **YES — one of the three.** The **too-quick denial**, arriving a beat before
  an honest answer could. The behaviour may recur more often than the literal phrase.
  **It must break in Ch.13 and must never be smooth again after Ch.20.**
- **THE CAP, corrected 2026-09-10 — and what it counts.** It read "capped at 5 (3 spent)",
  and the bookkeeping was wrong when it was written: the measured count is **5 of 5 already
  spent** — Ch.1 ×1 (*"Fine, Rx said, smooth again"*), Ch.3 ×3 (*"Fine"* in the transport bay,
  then *"I'm fine."* and *"I'm fine, Vexx"* in the same paragraph), Ch.9 ×1. That left Ch.13's
  mandated break with no budget, which is a cap forbidding the payoff it exists to protect.
  **The cap counts SMOOTH firings only.** The Ch.13 break is the device *failing*, not firing —
  a reflex that does not arrive, or arrives and is contradicted by the thing underneath it —
  and a broken tic is not the tic. **It does not count against the cap, and neither does any
  use after Ch.20, all of which must be broken ones.** So: five smooth uses spent, none left;
  every remaining appearance in this book must be a break.
  ⚠️ Count with `grep -n "\*Fine\*\|\*I'm fine" manuscript/chapters/*.md` before adding one
  — the phrase varies, and the last audit of it was wrong by hand.
- **Chaos markers:** Vexx's marksmanship tally, deployed at maximally wrong moments (Ch.8, 13,
  26) · minimisation by vocabulary · the rain that mutates into gravel and then into nothing ·
  losing interface control in the field (Ch.13) and a joke that stops mid-clause (Ch.20).
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** He is the only voice whose **speed** is the tell — Aglaope also
  under-answers, but she *pauses* first and Rx never does; his denial arrives before an honest
  answer could have been assembled, and that gap is the whole book.
- **Sample line:** *Fine. Processing hiccup. You should see the other guy.*

#### RICHARD JAMESON — commanding officer of the rebuilt Valkyr. The antagonist the reader must like.
> **GATE CONDITION.** No line of his may be readable as a threat, a double meaning, a knowing
> pause or an aside that is cleverer for the reader than for Vexx. **All the irony in this book
> belongs to the reader's later knowledge, never to his performance.** He does not know he has
> been made and he never will in this volume.
- **Attention / worldview:** Reduces the world to **outcomes and decisions.** A decision that
  worked was right; a decision that failed was wrong, regardless of process. He applies this
  identically to logistics, personnel and murder, and it presents as admirable pragmatism.
  Indecision is the only vice he recognises.
- **Vocabulary band:** Senior ONI, thirty-plus years, longevity access. **Concrete nouns and
  precise numbers; no adjectives of feeling.** He does not euphemise — he names the ugly thing
  plainly and is faintly impatient with institutions that don't.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **He pre-empts your objection and states it better than you would,
  then answers it.** At least once per Jameson scene. He gives power away in small deliberate
  increments (*"Drop the sir when it's just us"*) and every concession costs him nothing.
- **Evasion — never says:** *Unfortunate. Tragic. Regrettable.* He never softens a fact with a
  register word, and he never apologises for anything large — because large things have not
  occurred to him as the kind of thing one apologises for.
- **Physicality / silence:** **He stands when an operator enters a room.** He remembers a
  junior technician's name six weeks later. **He never raises his voice — not once in this
  book.** His one loss of control (Ch.10) is anger *on someone else's behalf*, lasting about
  eight seconds, followed by a sincere apology that is better than the anger was.
- **Verbal tic:** **NONE, deliberately.** A catchphrase would make him legible. His warmth *is*
  his signature and it must never harden into a formula.
- **Chaos markers:** the barometer — he is a fussy amateur weather-watcher and mentions local
  pressure everywhere he goes and nobody cares (Ch.10, 12, 24; **it must never become a
  metaphor**) · outcome grading · the four days, the unit that died on a board's timetable,
  surfacing obliquely for twenty chapters and told whole and truthfully in Ch.24 · the eight
  seconds of anger in Ch.10.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** He is the only voice that **improves your argument before
  defeating it** — Zeus tells you what you are *doing*, Jameson tells you what you are
  *thinking* and agrees with it — and the only character in the book whose kindness is entirely
  sincere and entirely compatible with a murder.
- **Sample line:** *"You're about to tell me the chain of custody was compromised, and you're
  right, and it was mine that compromised it. Now tell me what we do about the man."*

## THE CELL — OPERATORS

#### AGLAOPE — psych pressure / lure; Vexx's off-record confidante
- **Attention / worldview:** Reduces the world to *who in this room is carrying something.* She
  reads weight, not motive. Years of last-contact work — staying with the dying so none of them
  died alone — have made her a specialist in the physics of what people can hold.
- **Vocabulary band:** Plain, small, unadorned. Fewer words than anyone else in the cell. No
  jargon; she is the only operator whose speech carries almost no military register.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** **Statements with a long pause built into them.** She does not ask
  permission and she does not ask questions she already has the answer to. Short sentences,
  then silence she refuses to fill.
- **Evasion — never says:** *"Sorry."* Never *"it's okay."* Never offers reassurance, which is
  precisely why people believe her.
- **Physicality / silence:** **She reaches over and rests a hand on an arm.** One gesture, hers
  alone; nobody else in the book performs it. She is the most physically affectionate person in
  the cell and the one who least wants to be touched, and nobody has noticed because she is
  always the one reaching.
- **Verbal tic:** None — distinguished by worldview, evasion and one gesture.
- **Chaos markers:** the list of names of everyone she has sat with, which she *edits* and will
  argue about alphabetising (Ch.9, 16, 23 — in Ch.16 she adds the records clerk's name to it) ·
  responsibility inflation · a cold hand with a wedding band turned inward, mutating · laughing
  at the death notice in Ch.14 and being unable to stop for forty seconds.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** She and Gaia both state things flatly, but **Gaia states facts
  about the world and Aglaope states facts about you** — and she is the only character who
  names what she has noticed without being invited to.
- **Sample line:** *"You're carrying something. I don't need to know what it is. I just want you
  to know I noticed."*

#### GOLIATH — breach / demolitions; Vexx's steadying counterweight
- **Attention / worldview:** Reduces the world to *the job, and whether it was done honestly.*
  He sees charge patterns, load-bearing walls, and whether a man is straight with him. There is
  no third category for people.
- **Vocabulary band:** Trade language. Demolitions, ordnance, structures — and he applies it to
  ethics without noticing he is doing it. No abstractions, ever.
- **Syntax fingerprint:** Declaratives. Concrete nouns. **He asks one question and then waits,
  and the wait is longer than is comfortable.** *"Good"* as a complete sentence when something
  has been settled.
- **Evasion — never says:** A feeling word. He does not have the vocabulary and has never
  wanted it. He also never asks Vexx what he is carrying (Ch.18) — the refusal is the loyalty.
- **Physicality / silence:** He fills a doorway the way weather fills a sky, and **he goes
  silent when he is hurt**, which everyone reads as calm.
- **Verbal tic:** None — distinguished by trade vocabulary applied to moral positions.
- **Chaos markers:** the coffee pot and his system and the escalating unserious feud about it
  (Ch.9, 18, 25 — by Ch.25 he has given up on the system) · all-or-nothing on people, which
  breaks in Ch.18 when Vexx is lying to him *and* straight with him and he has no category for
  both · a colony street after rain, the morning the stranger left · snapping at Paladin about
  the coffee two days after a mission that had nothing to do with coffee.
- **DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE:** He and Paladin are the book's two moral voices, and they are
  separated by their *entry point* — **Paladin leads with the regulation and arrives at the
  ethics; Goliath leads with the craft** (*"there's a difference between clearing a threat and

## Maintenance rule

**The Writer adds an entry the moment a new named character first appears on the page, before
finalizing that chapter.** Written at introduction, a voice gets *designed*; written
retroactively at Chapter 24, it gets *described*, and by then the drift has already happened.
Every new entry must (a) end in a DISTINCTNESS GUARANTEE, (b) name its nearest neighbour in the
cast and the axis that separates them, and (c) **re-check the §TIC BUDGET** — the tic-bearer
roster is full at three, and every device in the map above is already owned.
