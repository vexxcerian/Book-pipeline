# sample-04 — RX DEFLECTION (the two-brother register)
**Type:** controlled / dialogue · **Source:** the author's own prose, Chapters One and Three
**BENCHMARK #3 — the tic in its unbroken state.**

---

You've gotten rusty, Rx told him, four days in, after Vexx had put a third consecutive round wide of a moving target.

I've been retired for a year.

Excuses. I died and I'm still a better shot than you.

*(Chapter Three, in the clearing:)*

Rx.

I'm fine.

Too quick again — the same reflex as the transport bay, the same reflex as Jameson's office three weeks earlier, but sharper now, more urgent, like something straining against a door that had held for months and had just, for one fractional second, not held quite as well.

I'm fine, Vexx. Focus on the operator. We'll talk about it after.

---

**What this is the benchmark for.**

- **The unquoted interface voice.** Rx speaks without quotation marks (italic in the author's original). Hold this convention manuscript-wide: it makes the most intimate presence in the book typographically *not in the room*.
- **Joke first, meaning second — and the meaning is the message.** *"I died and I'm still a better shot than you"* is how these two men say *I am still here.*
- **The tic, unbroken.** The too-quick denial arrives a beat before an honest answer could have been assembled. **It is one of only three permitted tics in the book, it is the seal talking rather than a speech habit, and the literal phrase is capped at 5 uses (3 already spent).**
- **The narration reads the timing, never the interior.** *"Too quick again"* is Vexx inferring from speed. Nothing here asserts what Rx feels, and nothing in the manuscript ever may.
- **Writer note:** the *narration* of the denial as `too quick` is itself at risk (2 uses). Vary the verb. The behaviour is the tic; the description of it is not.
