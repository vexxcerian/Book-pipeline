# sample-12 — THE TWO-HANDER (the empathy register)
**Type:** controlled / dialogue · **Source:** the author's own prose, Chapter Five

---

"Why didn't it worry you? That I might be — I don't know. Grieving my way into seeing something that isn't there."

"Because grief makes people see things that aren't there," Aglaope said, quiet, certain. "It doesn't usually make them this careful about proving themselves wrong first. You've spent months building a case you haven't shown anyone, instead of running straight at the first name that felt right. That's not grief talking. That's a man who wants to be certain before he lets himself be angry."

Vexx sat with that for a long moment, something in his chest loosening slightly for the first time in weeks — not resolution, nothing so clean, just the particular relief of finally being witnessed by someone who didn't need him to have an answer yet, only needed him to have said the thing out loud.

---

**What this is the benchmark for.** The book's **primary engagement lane is empathy (55%)**, and it lives almost entirely in two-handers built like this one.

**Rules it demonstrates:**
- **Aglaope states facts about *you*, unasked, without softening.** She does not reassure, does not say *sorry*, does not say *it's okay*. That refusal is why she is believed. Compare Gaia, who states facts about the *world*.
- **The narration qualifies the feeling downward rather than upward.** *"not resolution, nothing so clean"* — the voice reaches for a large word and refuses it. That refusal is the register.
- **Still no emotional temperature report.** *"something in his chest loosening slightly"* is a physical event, not a named emotion. The line between those two things is the hardest rule in this book and this sentence sits exactly on the right side of it.
- **Pacing:** empathy chapters run *short* and *slow*. Fascination chapters (missions, investigation) run *long* and *fast*. **Alternate the load by chapter; never blend it evenly.**
- One instance of *"the particular ___"* again. Ration it in new chapters.
