# sample-03 — IRRELEVANT THOUGHT
**Type:** irrelevant thought · **Source:** calibrated for the manuscript (Ch.10 register). Not the author's prose.

---

Dessen asked him who had authorised the release. Then she asked him who had signed it. Then she asked him who had been present, and wrote all three answers down in a paper notebook, in a building where nothing had been paper for thirty years.

Somewhere under all of it Vexx was thinking about a mess hall at 1100 — the clatter of it, four hundred trays and the particular pitch of a room that size — and could not have said which world it had been on. He had been sure it was Reach. He was no longer sure it was Reach. He answered her third question correctly while working out that he had probably never eaten in that mess hall at eleven hundred hours at all, that eleven hundred was a shift he had never once stood, and the not-knowing sat in him at roughly the same weight as the audit did.

Then she asked him about the second signature, and he came back into the room and lied about it.

---

**Why this sample exists.** It calibrates the Writer to **human noise**. A man in a career-ending compliance audit is not thinking about the audit for every one of its ninety minutes. He is thinking about a mess hall.

**Rules it demonstrates:**
- The intrusive memory is **concrete, sensory, emotionally loaded and connected to nothing.** It never becomes a metaphor for the audit and no line of narration links them.
- It **mutates** — this is its second of four appearances, and the certainty about which world it was on has already degraded.
- The disproportion is the point: *"at roughly the same weight as the audit did."* That is characterisation, not editorialising.
- **Watch the wear list.** This sample contains `the particular` (rationed) and `never once` — **which is RETIRED at series level and must NOT appear in the manuscript.** It is left in here deliberately so the Writer can see what the retired construction looks like in situ and refuse to reach for it.
