# sample-05 — JAMESON (the gate condition, demonstrated)
**Type:** dialogue · **Source:** calibrated for the manuscript (Ch.10 register). Not the author's prose.

---

"You're about to tell me the chain of custody was compromised," Jameson said, "and you're right. It was. You're also about to tell me that the compromise originated with an operator pulling material out of evidence lock on his own authorisation, which is also right, and which is the part I'd be writing up if I were you." He turned a page without hurrying. "What I'd want somebody to ask me, if I were the one holding the notebook, is which of the two possible failures I'd rather have on this program's file. A cell that mishandled a chain of custody. Or a cell that read a man's account, believed him, and did nothing about it because the paperwork was cleaner that way."

Dessen said, "That isn't the question in front of me, sir."

"No," Jameson agreed. "It isn't. It's the one I'd have wanted somebody to put in front of me, and nobody did, and I've thought about it for a long time since." He closed the file. "Log the finding. Log my objection under it. I'd rather you had both."

---

**Why this sample exists.** It is the **gate condition** made concrete: *a scene where the reader can see he is the villain is a failed scene.*

**Rules it demonstrates:**
- **He pre-empts the objection and states it better than the person holding it**, then answers it. His device, nobody else's, at least once per Jameson scene.
- **He does not pull rank and he does not raise his voice.** He concedes the procedural point completely and moves the argument to ground where he is right.
- **He is honest.** He asks for his own objection to be logged. This costs him nothing and it is also entirely sincere, and both things are true at once.
- **The oblique fragment of the four days** — *"nobody did, and I've thought about it for a long time since"* — surfaces without explanation, twenty chapters before it is told whole in Ch.24.
- **No double meaning. No knowing pause. No line cleverer for the reader than for Vexx.** Check every Jameson line against that. If it has a chill in it, the chill must come from a *document*, never from him.
- **Dessen is correct and is not a stooge.** *"That isn't the question in front of me, sir."*
