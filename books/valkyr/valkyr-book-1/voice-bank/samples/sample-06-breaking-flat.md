# sample-06 — BREAKING (the primary break: the prose goes FLAT)
**Type:** breaking · **Source:** the author's own prose, Chapter One
**BENCHMARK #2 — the voice at maximum pressure.**

---

They buried an empty casket. Protocol, for classified KIAs — remains unable to be recovered from the field. Vexx stood over the box that held nothing, in a dress uniform he hadn't worn since his own discharge, sun too bright for the occasion, some chaplain's voice going on about sacrifice and duty in a cadence that could have belonged to any funeral for any soldier the war had eaten that month. Vexx didn't hear most of it. He stood there and thought, with a clarity that frightened him more than any firefight ever had: there was never going to be anything in there. There's nothing to bury. There's just a box shaped like an apology, and I'm standing here accepting it because there's nothing else on offer.

---

**How this voice breaks: it goes FLAT.** Not fragmented. Not lyrical. **Underdescriptive.**

- **The five-word paragraph opening at the highest-load moment in the book.** *"They buried an empty casket."* The long accumulating mode stops; the narration reports physical facts in sequence like a log entry.
- **Present-tense leakage.** *"There's nothing to bury."* Past-tense narration slips into present for two sentences at maximum load and slips back with no comment and no signalling.
- **Direct-address collapse.** *"…and I'm standing here accepting it"* — the narration slides into Vexx's unmarked interior first person and out again. This is the deepest register the book has and it should be used **three or four times in the whole manuscript**, no more.
- *"a box shaped like an apology"* is a CAPPED motif: **2 uses total, whole book.** This is the first. The second and last is Chapter 26.
