# sample-02 — CONTROLLED (institutional register)
**Type:** controlled · **Source:** the author's own prose, Chapter Four

---

"Which has been reviewed and assessed as inconclusive." The officer didn't look up from her data pad. "This matter is closed, Operator Vexxcerian. Your cell's report reflects well on your unit's capability. That's the outcome that matters here."

Vexx sat with that for a long moment, turning it over the way he'd turned over Corwin's shaking hands in the jungle, his hollowed-out eleven-day stare, the certainty in his voice describing an order given clean and practiced by someone who'd done this before.

Inconclusive was doing an enormous amount of work in that sentence, and Vexx had spent enough of his life around people who chose their words for a living to recognize deliberate softness when he heard it.

---

**What to take from it.**

- **Institutional vocabulary is used correctly and without irony by the narration.** The narration does not sneer at *inconclusive*. It reads it, accurately, the way an auditor would — and the irony is manufactured entirely in the reader.
- **This is the book's identity effect in one paragraph.** The reader is being taught a skill they will use for the next twenty-two chapters and, if the book works, in their own working life afterward.
- **The tricolon of physical recall** (shaking hands / eleven-day stare / the certainty in his voice) is the catalogue move — physical facts listed in place of a feeling.
- Note *"the way he'd turned over…"* — the `the way X does Y` construction runs at 1.22/1k in the draft. **At risk. Do not exceed 1.5/1k.**
