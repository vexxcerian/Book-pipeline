# Voice Bank — Valkyr: Book One

> **What this is.** Thirteen short calibration samples the Writer reads *before* drafting and
> the Editor measures *against*. They are the voice in its four operating modes: at rest, under
> pressure, in dialogue, and carrying human noise.
>
> **The spec these samples serve is `voice-dna.md`.** This bank is the audible version of it.
> Where a sample and the spec disagree, the spec wins.
>
> **Provenance matters and is labelled on every file.** Five samples are **the author's own
> prose**, quoted from `research/original-draft.md` — those are the ground truth. Eight are
> **calibrated for the manuscript**: written to the spec, demonstrating a move the drafted
> chapters had no occasion to make yet. A calibrated sample is a *target*, not a precedent; if
> it ever contradicts the author's prose, the author's prose is right.

---

## The voice in one paragraph

Plain Anglo-Saxon nouns and precise verbs, at vocabulary level 6/10. **Bimodal rhythm** —
34.6% of the draft's sentences are six words or shorter and 11.2% are forty or longer, and the
voice does not do *medium*. Feeling is never named; it is displaced onto an object, a
procedure or a physical fact. A retrospective narrator who knows more than the scene, used
sparingly. Institutional vocabulary handled correctly and without irony, so the reader supplies
the irony. Humour is dry, sparse and almost entirely Rx's, and its job is to prove two men
share a private language. **Under maximum pressure the prose goes FLAT** — not fragmented, not
lyrical, underdescriptive — and occasionally slips into present tense or into Vexx's unmarked
first person and back out without comment.

## How to use this bank

**Before drafting a chapter:** read benchmark 1, plus whichever of 06/07/08/13 matches the
chapter's pressure level, plus 05 if Jameson is in it and 11 if more than two AIs speak.

**After drafting, before finalising:**

1. **Run the sentence histogram.** ≥25% of sentences at ≤6 words AND ≥8% at ≥40. **A fat middle
   is off-voice** no matter how good the prose is. Check this before anything else.
2. Zero emotional temperature reports. Zero metacognition. Zero explanatory similes.
3. Similes ≤3.5/1k · em-dashes ~8/1k in new chapters · `the particular ___` ≤0.25/1k.
4. Did anyone acquire a tic who is not **Rx, Echo or Spector**? Cut it.
5. Did counting, listing, rating or tallying appear in anyone but **Spector**? Cut it.
6. Are `filed it away`, `not yet a problem`, `a shape on the horizon`, `the vocabulary to name
   it` or `never once` anywhere in the chapter? Remove them — all five are **CLOSED**.
7. Does any sentence know what Rx feels? Rewrite it as inference from voice, timing or cursor.
8. Is there a Jameson line cleverer for the reader than for Vexx? Delete it.
9. Cover the names on every line of dialogue. If you cannot tell who is speaking, open
   `character-bible.md`.
10. New named character? **Add their card to `character-bible.md` before finalising**, and
    re-check the §TIC BUDGET — the roster is full at three and every device is already owned.

## Deliberate traps in this bank

Three samples contain a construction the manuscript is **forbidden** to use, left in place so
the Writer learns to recognise it in situ rather than in a rule list:

- **`sample-03`** and **`sample-10`** both contain **`never once`** — **RETIRED at series
  level** (the Amelia Lesson). It reads perfectly naturally in both places. That is exactly why
  it is dangerous. Rewrite it wherever the reflex produces it.
- **`sample-01`**, **`sample-03`** and **`sample-12`** each contain **`the particular ___`** —
  the author's most calcified construction, running at 0.61/1k in the draft. It is a genuine
  signature and it is over-used. New chapters get roughly one per 4,000 words.

**Do not sand any of this out of the drafted Chapters 1–5.** Chapter 1 is locked; Chapters 2–5
are the author's at his own standard. The budgets above apply to **new** chapters, and exist so
the *book-level* mechanical gates pass without anyone touching the author's prose.

## Standing prohibitions (gate conditions, not preferences)

| Banned | Why |
|---|---|
| Any rampancy vocabulary — timers, degradation rates, "how long does he have" | STATE.yaml guardrail. The easiest unearned tension to import from Halo canon. Rx's instability is memory suppression, trauma and moral weight, full stop. |
| Any sentence asserting Rx's interior state | POV is Vexx, close third, whole book. No Rx POV, no Jameson POV. |
| Any Jameson line readable as threat, double meaning or knowing aside | A scene where the reader can see he is the villain is a failed scene. |
| Any hard in-universe calendar date | Era only: early-to-mid Human-Covenant War, four years after Rx's death. |
| Narration glossing RECLAMATION on its first appearance (Ch.7) | Kills the book's primary re-read reward. |
| Narration connecting Vexx's *filing* habit to Corwin's *"they file you"* (Ch.19) | The reader makes it. If the book makes it, the best line in Part Three dies. |
